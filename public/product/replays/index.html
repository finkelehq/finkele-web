3D flood replays

A solver run of a real storm, played through time in 3D, showing where the water goes and when it gets there. A simulation of the storm, not a forecast.

Status and limits

Status

Private preview

In private preview. Book a demo to see it for your sites.

What it does not do yet

  • A simulation of the storm, not a forecast, and not compared with observed flooding.
  • Rain only in the Exeter replay: drains and the River Exe's own flow are not modelled.
  • Replays with a river channel are being re-run after a fix to how the channel bed was set, and carry a caveat until then.
  • Heights are exaggerated so the ground reads: three times in the Exeter film.

Specification

What you get

What it is
One LISFLOOD-FP solver run of a real storm, with water depth saved through time, not only the peak.
View
3D, played through time.
Rainfall
The storm as recorded by Environment Agency rain gauges: three gauges for Exeter.
Ground
Environment Agency LiDAR terrain, with Ordnance Survey OpenMap Local building footprints as solid blocks.
Cells
2 m in the Exeter replay.
First replay
Exeter city centre, 17 September 2023.

How it works

How it works

  1. Step 1

    Recorded rain

    The storm's rainfall as Environment Agency gauges recorded it.

  2. Step 2

    One solver run

    LISFLOOD-FP runs the storm over the area's terrain and buildings, saving depth through time.

  3. Step 3

    Replay

    The frames play in 3D, so you can see where water gathers and when.

Data inputs

What goes into it

  • Ground

    Terrain

    Environment Agency LiDAR on a 1 m grid: the bare-earth ground the water runs over.

  • On the ground

    Buildings

    Ordnance Survey OpenMap Local footprints, standing as solid blocks where a solver run models buildings, as in the replays.

  • The storm

    Recorded rainfall

    Rain as recorded by Environment Agency gauges during the storm.

  • The simulation

    One solver run

    A LISFLOOD-FP run of that one storm, saving water depth through time.

A replay shows the solver itself, not TERRA.
