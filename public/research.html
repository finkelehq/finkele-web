Research

Research

Three lines of work on our flood model that are not in the product yet. None of these research candidates is the model we serve. Every figure keeps its depth threshold, its areas and its storms.

Before you read

How to read this page

Verification and validation

Verification compares a model with the solver it learned from, on the same area and storm. Validation compares it with a flood that was observed. Everything here is verification, or a check of a model against itself.

CSI

The critical success index scores agreement between two flood maps: cells both call wet, divided by cells either calls wet. 1 is a perfect match and 0 is no overlap. A cell is wet when its water is at least the stated depth.

Held-out storms

Three held-out synthetic storms of rising size run on test areas at Cambridge, Manchester and Hebden Bridge: ho1 is 40 mm over 3 h, ho2 80 mm over 12 h and ho3 150 mm over 24 h.

Research topic 1 of 3

Depth at 1 m across whole 5 km areas

On ten test areas it had never seen, on one storm (storm 49, 291.8 mm over 7.5 h), the v2 candidate scored a CSI of 0.571 at 15 cm against the solver. On the same cells the model we serve today scored 0.326, and a terrain-only threshold 0.284. On the nine held-out runs it scored 0.515, the served model 0.213 and the threshold 0.269.

What it does not show yet

  • In Hebden Bridge's steep valley the served model is ahead on all three held-out storms: 0.372 to 0.402 at 15 cm, against v2's 0.209 to 0.318.
  • Water in features only a few metres wide, such as a steep, narrow valley floor, is not yet resolved.
  • The served model was trained on different solver runs, so these figures compare two products, not two designs.
  • v2 is not promoted to the product, and no AXIOM certificate has been run on it.

What it predicts and how it was checked

From the terrain and a storm's rainfall, the model predicts the peak water depth for every 1 m cell across a whole 5 km area. It trained on 53 solver runs over 22 areas and was scored against physics solver runs at 1 m on the same areas and storms, on tests written down before training.

Research topic 2 of 3

Testing that moving the grid does not move the answer

With the model's grid moved 250 m on both axes, the answer barely changed: at most 2 of about 21 million cells per area switched between wet and dry at 15 cm, on ten test areas and storm 49, against a pre-registered limit of 1% of cells. Its predecessor v1 switched 2.18% on average and failed that limit; the model we serve today switched 10.59% on average.

Since 20 October 2026 a model must also pass a stricter test before it can be promoted: 24 windows on 22 sites, the grid moved 250 cells, no more than 0.001% of cells may switch at 5, 15 or 30 cm, and the 99th percentile of depth change must be at most 1 mm. The model we serve today fails every window, with 1.48% to 13.70% of cells switching at 5 cm. A research model built so that moving the grid changes nothing, which is not served either, passes with no cell switching on any window.

What it does not show yet

  • This is a check of the model against itself: both sides are the model, so it is neither verification nor validation.
  • Promoting v2 would still need the promotion test and the AXIOM gate.
  • The promotion test's pass rule is still to be confirmed.

How it was checked

The same area is predicted twice, with the grid where it is used and with it moved, and the two maps are compared cell by cell.

Research topic 3 of 3

Water through time

On the nine held-out runs, the time-resolved candidate scored a CSI of 0.543 at 15 cm, pooled over every saved frame, against 0.402 for v2's peak map held flat through each storm. It gains most by being right early in a storm, while the water is still spreading.

What it does not show yet

  • It missed its pre-registered timing test: the time of the largest flooded area was within 1 hour of the solver's on 6 of 9 runs, where 7 were needed. On the 24-hour storm it was 3.0 hours late at Cambridge and 1.5 hours late at Manchester.
  • It floods too little late in long storms. At Manchester on the 24-hour storm, its largest flooded area at 15 cm was 229 ha, against the solver's 275 ha.
  • Every run it trained on is on lowland, so steep ground is outside what it has seen.
  • The 3-hour storm has the shape of a storm it trained on, scaled up; the 12-hour and 24-hour storms are new to it.
  • It is a prototype: not promoted, and no AXIOM certificate has been run on it.

What it predicts and how it was checked

For each moment in a storm, the model predicts the water depth from the terrain and the rain that has fallen up to that time. Each frame is compared with the solver's own frame, saved every 30 minutes.
