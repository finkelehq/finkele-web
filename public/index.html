The Problem

£3.3bn

in annual flood damage across the UK, projected to rise to £4.5bn by 2050. Many asset owners still lack the clear, local information they need to see flood risk at their own sites, street by street, and act early, before costly damage to property and operations occurs.

Source: CCC, A Well-Adapted UK; 2025 prices, 2°C warming.

The Solution

In seconds

Our model maps water depth in metres across a catchment from a storm's rainfall, so owners can see in advance which streets and buildings it reaches, and how deep it gets. It works from the latest rainfall forecast and your location, so it is as early as that forecast.

Measured: 8.5 s per 1 km square in Leeds, 2-CPU service.

Our technology

AI trained on physics simulations

TERRA is our proprietary AI flood model. We run a physics-based hydraulic solver through many storms over Environment Agency LiDAR terrain, and TERRA learns from those runs. Give it a storm's rainfall and it returns water depth in metres, with an uncertainty band, in seconds.

  • Fast

    Seconds for a 1 km square

    8.5 s of measured inference on our deployed service, so each new rainfall forecast becomes a depth map while it is still current.

  • Detailed

    1 m grid

    Depth on a 1 m grid over Environment Agency LiDAR, resolving water street by street, with an uncertainty band on every cell.

  • Across England

    Over 750 areas registered

    Registered on Environment Agency LiDAR across England, with more being added and new areas set up when you ask for them.

Inputs

What goes into a Finkele flood forecast

Four inputs and the model that uses them. Each is named, so you can see what a depth rests on.

  • Ground

    Terrain

    Environment Agency LiDAR on a 1 m grid: the bare-earth ground the water runs over, street by street.

  • On the ground

    Buildings

    Ordnance Survey footprints, as solid blocks where a solver run models buildings, as in our replays.

  • The storm

    Rainfall

    Forecast rain for coming storms, gauge records for replays, and design storms for scenarios.

  • Training data

    Solver runs

    Physics solver runs over many storms on real terrain: the training data our model learns from.

  • The model

    TERRA

    Our AI model, trained on those runs, gives water depth in metres, with an uncertainty band.

The forecast model's terrain is bare earth today, without buildings or flood defences. It uses rain falling on the area only, not river or coastal flow.

Evidence and limits

What we check, and what it does not do yet

What we check and how

Comparing TERRA with the solver's own runs is verification. Comparing it with recorded floods is validation. We report the two separately.

  • Verification. How often the physics solver's depth falls inside our uncertainty band. This coverage is being re-measured on the band we now ship, so we give no figure until that is done.
  • Validation. Comparison with recorded floods is open work.

What it does not do yet

  • No return periods: each map is for a stated rainfall.
  • Bare-earth terrain: buildings removed, flood defences not included.
  • Peak depth, not the time the water arrives.
  • Rain falling on the area only, not river or coastal flooding.
  • Checking results against satellite images will come later.
