Environment Agency warnings

The Environment Agency's flood warnings for each area, in their own words with a link to GOV.UK, shown beside our forecast but kept apart.

What it does not do yet

  • Our model is surface water from rainfall. Environment Agency warnings are mostly for rivers and the sea, so they answer a different question.
  • The Environment Agency's terms say its data is not for safety-critical use. For safety decisions, use GOV.UK and the Environment Agency's own services.
  • A new or redrawn Environment Agency area is matched at the next monthly refresh.

Specification

What you get

Warnings
Every Environment Agency flood warning or flood alert for an area that overlaps yours.
Wording
The Environment Agency's label word for word, the area's name, and when it was raised or last changed.
Link
The area's GOV.UK Check for flooding page.
Kept apart
In its own block, headed as the Environment Agency's, separate from our forecast.
None in force
Said in so many words, with the time we checked. Never shown as an all-clear.
Map layer
Optional and off by default: draws the areas with a warning in force.
River gauges
Levels only: the reading, its time and the typical range.

How it works

How it works

  1. Step 1

    Match the areas

    The Environment Agency's flood warning and alert areas are matched to each area we serve, from the Agency's own records.

  2. Step 2

    Read the warnings

    Warnings in force come from the Environment Agency's flood-monitoring API, which the Agency updates every 15 minutes.

  3. Step 3

    Show them apart

    They appear beside our forecast, in the Agency's words, never merged into it.
