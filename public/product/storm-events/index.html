Storm event pages

One page per storm: the areas its forecast rain reaches, our forecast for each, and a scorecard once it has passed. Storm Austen is the first.

Book a demo What goes into a forecast

Status and limits

Status

Private preview

In private preview. Book a demo to see it for your sites.

What it does not do yet

  • It is as early as the rainfall forecast it is given.
  • The scorecard compares our forecast with solver runs. That is verification; comparison with recorded flooding (validation) is open work.
  • No return periods: each forecast is for the storm's forecast rain.
  • Rain falling on each area only, not river or coastal flooding.

Specification

What you get

One page per storm
Opened when a named storm's forecast rain reaches the areas we serve.
Each area
Our flood forecast: peak depth in metres with its band, and when the rainfall forecast was issued.
Warnings
The Environment Agency's warnings beside each area, kept apart from our forecast (how).
Scorecard
Once the storm has passed, each area's forecast is set beside solver runs at three depths: 0.05 m, 0.15 m and 0.30 m.
Flooded, defined
An area counts as flooded at a depth when at least 1% of its land is at that depth or deeper. The rule was fixed before Storm Austen's final scores were read.
First storm
Storm Austen.

How it works

How it works

  1. Step 1

    Forecast rain

    The storm's forecast rain over each area, with its issue time.

  2. Step 2

    TERRA

    Peak depth for each area the rain reaches, with its band.

  3. Step 3

    Scorecard

    After the storm, the forecast is scored against solver runs, at the three depths.

Data inputs

What goes into it

  • Ground

    Terrain

    Environment Agency LiDAR on a 1 m grid: the bare-earth ground the water runs over.

  • The storm

    Rainfall forecast

    Forecast rain for the coming days over each area, from Open-Meteo.

  • Training data

    Solver runs

    Physics solver runs over many storms on real terrain: the physics-based training data the model learns from.

  • The model

    TERRA

    Our AI model, trained on those runs. From a storm's rainfall it gives water depth in metres, with an uncertainty band.
