The Problem

£3.3bn

in annual flood damage across the UK, projected to rise to £4.5bn by 2050. Many asset owners lack the information they need to see flood risk at their sites before damage occurs.

Source: Climate Change Committee, A Well-Adapted UK; 2025 prices, 2050 under 2°C of global warming.

The Solution

In seconds

Finkele's flood model maps water depth in metres across a catchment from a storm's rainfall, so owners can see which streets and buildings it reaches. It works from the rainfall forecast and the location, so it is as early as that forecast.

Measured inference time on our deployed service: 8.5 s for a 1 km square.

How it works

From terrain to depth in metres

  1. Step 1

    Terrain

    Environment Agency 1 m LiDAR terrain.

  2. Step 2

    Solver runs

    LISFLOOD-FP solver runs over many storms.

  3. Step 3

    TERRA

    A machine-learning model trained on those runs, which gives depth in metres from a storm's rainfall.

    shallowdeep

It is as early as the rainfall forecast it is given.

Evidence and limits

What we check, and what it does not do yet

What we check and how

Comparing TERRA's depths with the solver's own runs is verification. Comparing them with floods that were recorded is validation. We report the two separately.

  • Verification. How often the solver's depth falls inside our uncertainty band. This coverage is being re-measured on the band we now ship, so we give no figure yet.
  • Validation. Comparison with recorded floods is open work.

What it does not do yet

  • No return periods: each map is for a stated rainfall.
  • Terrain is bare earth with buildings removed, and flood defences are not included.
  • Peak depth, not the time the water arrives.
  • Rain falling on the area only, not river or coastal flooding.
  • Checking results against satellite images will come later.
